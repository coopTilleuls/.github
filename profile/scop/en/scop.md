# 🏛 How our cooperative works

## What is a Scop?

A *Société coopérative et participative* (Scop) is a French worker cooperative: its employees are the majority members, holding at least 51% of the capital and 65% of the voting rights. Not every employee has to be a member, but every employee is expected to become one.

Like any company, a Scop has managers, but they are elected by the members. Profits are shared between:

- employees, through profit-sharing schemes;
- the company’s reserves, which can never be distributed and strengthen its equity over the long term.

Members are paid for their work. Their capital contribution is paid back when they leave, with no capital gain.

## Our cooperative in practice

Les-Tilleuls.coop is a *SARL Scop* (a limited liability worker cooperative), 99.9% owned by its employees. Each year, 84% of the profits are shared among employees and 16% go into reserves (legal reserve and development fund). Shares earn no dividend.

## Becoming a member

Every employee on a permanent contract has one year from their start date, once their probation period is over, to apply for membership. The process is:

1. a written application is sent to the administrative director before the annual general meeting;
2. the application is voted on at the start of the annual general meeting, and the new member can vote at that same meeting;
3. once accepted, the new member buys one €20 share.

After that, 2% of their gross salary is set aside each month to build up their capital, until it reaches the equivalent of two months’ gross salary.

# 🗳 Governance

## The annual general meeting

The annual general meeting takes place once a year, usually in June, within six months of the end of the financial year. It is where we:

- review the past year and how the cooperative is running;
- examine and approve the accounts;
- decide how the profits are allocated;
- vote on new members;
- set the main strategic directions.

An extraordinary general meeting is called for decisions that affect the articles of association or the structure of the cooperative: capital, legal form, merger, dissolution… Its decisions require a qualified majority. At both annual and extraordinary general meetings, only members vote.

We also meet about twice a month, on site and online, in “informal” general meetings. We review the budget, sales, recruitment, events and our CSR indicators. These meetings have no legal standing, and everyone in the cooperative votes, employees, apprentices and interns alike. People who have announced they are leaving can attend but no longer vote. Minutes are written after every meeting.

Between meetings, decisions that require a vote (budgets, recruitment, taking part in initiatives…) are made remotely on [Loomio](https://www.loomio.coop/).

## Management

The management sets the cooperative’s strategic direction. Since June 2021, it has been a mixed co-management, currently Marion Agé and Kévin Dunglas (re-elected in June 2024). Major decisions are still made at general meetings: the co-managers hold a binding, revocable mandate to carry them out. The mandate lasts four years and can be renewed or revoked by the members. Kévin Dunglas was the sole manager from the cooperative’s founding in 2011 until 2021.

## The supervisory board

A supervisory board is mandatory in Scops with more than 20 members. It has 3 to 9 members, elected by the general meeting from among the members for three years. At least two thirds must be employee members, and the role cannot be held by a manager.

Our supervisory board members are: Inès Azzouzi, Cécile Ropital, Antoine Bluchet, Arnaud Frézet, Justine Zahiri and Thibaut Arnoud.

## The works council (CSE)

The *comité social et économique* (CSE) represents employees. It gives them a voice on decisions about the company’s management and economic development, work organization, professional training and working conditions. Its members are elected for four years.

Our CSE members are Laury Sorriaux, Vincent Fuger, Justine Delalleau, Clément Talleu, Margaux Feslard and Antoine Cau.
